Research Question

Describe your research question here. Include why this question might be of interest.

Our research question, ‘To what extent can variation in bike demand throughout the year in Seoul be attributed to changes in weather?’ aims to explore and model how different weather related factors affect fluctuations in bike demand in Seoul from the given data frame. This question might be of interest to local governing bodies when designing infrastructure or bike sharing companies that might want to optimise pricing and distribution of bikes.

Assumptions and Limitations:

When importing the dataset from excel into the R Studio project, the Date variable data type was converted into string rather than retaining its original format, so we performed data cleaning and transformation (in the form of as.Date()) to ensure all variables were suitable for analysis and effective plotting of data.

Since we decided to measure the influence of predictors on a bike demand (a numerical variable), we were limited to using linear regression when it came to modelling as logistic regression is only suitable for predicting categorical variables.

During the exploration of the data it seemed there might be some anomalous values in our outcome variable, such as several days with a 0 rented bike count at all hours or some extremely high counts at some hours. However, after reviewing a boxplot below of all hourly rented bike counts, we decided to keep these values in our analysis as it would not make sense to remove the high bike counts.

Both graphs illustrate the relationship between number of rented bikes and temperature throughout the year, except that Figure 1 examines the overall trend across the whole year whereas Figure 2 separates the data into four seasons, allowing seasonal variations to be explored and viewed in greater detail. Both graphs show that generally as temperature increases, so does bike demand implying that there is a relationship between warmer weather and greater demand for rental bikes.

Although the graphs suggest an association, this does not prove that temperature directly influences bike rental demand as correlation does not imply causation. Additionally, both graphs follow a similar trend, peaking in the summer (in the middle of the year) with either end of the year dipping to lower bike rental counts and lower temperatures. Both graphs also show a peak of both bike rentals and temperature during the summer period, but this is particularly visible in the initial graph; yet from this graph there is a distinct difference in the peaks between bike rentals and temperature, suggesting that temperature does not fully explain the variation in bike rental demand. The second graph breaks down the data in 4 segments to get a more precise reading and trend per season, showing a clear and distinct positive relationship in spring and more. The Pearson correlation coefficient for Figure 1 was calculated to be 0.69982 to 5 significant figures, suggesting a moderately strong linear relationship between the two variables, supporting the relationship deduced from the first graph; however the value is not strong enough for temperature to be the only variable affecting bike rental demand.
